Operator Guide

CORE Operator Handbook

How to take a construction PDF from a cold open to a priced, exported takeoff — and exactly where to stop and check before the number reaches a bid.

Written 24 Aug 2026 Build d4ee99b Service on 127.0.0.1:8100

01What this tool does

It reads a construction drawing, traces the linework, works out the scale, and turns what it found into a priced list of line items you can export.

It does not replace you. It replaces the part of the job where you trace every line by hand with a mouse. What you get instead is a sheet that arrives already measured, which you then correct — untick what isn't yours, fix the names, set the prices, and check the handful of things listed in Before you bid.

Everything the software decided is visible and reversible. Nothing is hidden behind a black box: every line type it found is a row you can see, tick, rename, or throw away.

The one thing to understand

Read this first

The sheet does not start empty. It starts full.

The moment a page finishes detecting, most line types are already ticked into your takeoff and already named. Your job is subtractive: read what is already on, and turn off what isn't yours.

The exact rule the software uses to decide what starts ticked:

Kind of lineStartsWhy
Any dashed or patterned lineONDashed linework is almost always something being measured — pipe, contour, fence, easement.
Solid lines longer than 500 points totalONLong solid runs are usually real work. Short ones are usually drawing furniture.
Solid lines shorter than thatoffTick marks, arrowheads, leader stubs.
Near-white lineworkoffReal ink, but almost always faint existing-conditions background. One click brings it in.
Hatch and texture fillsoffHatch stroke length is not a quantity.
Text, labels and leadersoffLeader lines used to get welded into wall tallies. They start off now.

Names work the same way. Every row arrives carrying a name the software proposed — read off the sheet's own legend where there is one, otherwise off the CAD layer name. That proposed name is what gets grouped, what gets a trade guessed for it, what a price attaches to, and what gets exported.

Consequence

An operator who types nothing still approves a bid with software-proposed line-item names on it, filed under trades that were guessed from those names by keyword. Read the names before you approve. They are usually right — but "usually" is doing real work in that sentence.

02Getting it running

Two things have to be up. From the project folder:

bash detection-engine/run-service.sh

Then open http://127.0.0.1:8100 in a browser.

Use the numbers, not the name

Type 127.0.0.1, not localhost. On this machine localhost resolves the slow way first and adds roughly two seconds to every single request, against a server that normally answers in about five milliseconds. If the app feels sluggish on every click, check the address bar before you check anything else.

Opening a drawing

Either pick from the Open sample plan… dropdown, or paste a full path into the …or paste a PDF path box and press Open. The page dropdown then fills in, and Detect lines appears.

Each page is labelled with what kind of drawing it is: vector means the PDF still carries real CAD geometry (the strongest case — layer names and exact coordinates survive), hybrid means a mix, and a scanned page means there is nothing but pixels and everything has to be read out of the image.

03A sheet, start to finish

This is the whole loop. Everything after this section is detail on one step of it.

  1. Detect the page

    Pick the page, press Detect lines. A first run on a big civil sheet takes roughly a minute and a half; after that the result is cached and comes back instantly.

    When it finishes, the status line tells you what it found — something like 1840 lines in 280 styles · scale 1 inch = 30 ft.

  2. Check the scale before anything else

    Every length on the sheet is multiplied by this one number, so a wrong scale is wrong everywhere at once and looks completely normal. See Scale.

    If the chip says the printed caption matches drawn bar, two independent sources agree and you can move on. If it doesn't, stop and resolve it now.

  3. Cull the line styles

    Work down the LINE STYLES panel. Untick everything that isn't your scope. Use the filter box to find things fast, and click a colour chip to isolate a whole colour at once.

    If you only do certain trades, declare them and let the app do the first pass of this for you.

  4. Fix the names

    The names came from the sheet's legend or its CAD layers. Correct any that are wrong or meaningless — the name is what groups rows together, what attracts a price, and what appears on the export.

    A name you type is remembered for that line type and comes back automatically on every other sheet in the document.

  5. Switch anything you price by the piece to EA

    The LF / EA button on each row decides whether that line type bills by footage or by count.

  6. Run any calculators the sheet needs

    Earthwork for cut and fill, Trench for pipe excavation, Structure for building footprints, Count for repeated symbols. Each writes extra rows into the takeoff. Read the volumes section first — these are the numbers most likely to be badly wrong.

  7. Check the itemized table and set prices

    Check the trade each row landed under, check the quantities, then type your unit rates. The extended totals fill in as you go.

  8. Approve & export

    CSV pulls the sheet into a spreadsheet. Approve & export sends it to the service and unlocks Report PDF (all approved pages).

    Do not skip

    If you typed over any footage quantity by hand, the CSV is your deliverable, not the report PDF. See the first item in Before you bid.

04Scale

One number turns points on paper into feet on the ground. Get it wrong and every length, area and volume on the sheet is wrong by the same factor, silently.

How the software finds it

It reads the printed caption (1" = 30', SCALE: 1:20, and so on), and separately measures any drawn scale bar by its tick marks. When both agree you get a chip reading ✓ matches drawn bar.

The only real confirmation

✓ matches drawn bar means two independent sources agreed. A green chip on its own does not mean verified — it can mean one printed caption was found and nothing contradicted it.

When a sheet prints several scales

A details sheet may carry a dozen. The app lists every scale it found and lets you switch with one click. It ranks them by which one governs the most drawing area, but on a mixed sheet — a plan plus a strip of details — no single number is right for the whole page. Take the plan off at the plan's scale and the details separately.

Typing a scale by hand

You can type one in. Two things to know:

  • A bare number means feet per paper inch. Typing 20 gives you 1 inch = 20 feet. Typing 0.25 gives you a quarter of a foot per inch, which is not quarter-inch scale.
  • The app refuses absurd values, but the band is wide — anything from about a tenth of a foot to a thousand feet per inch is accepted. A plausible typo is not caught. Typing 200 when you meant 20 sails straight through and makes the sheet ten times too big.
Not to scale

When a sheet prints NOT TO SCALE, the app reports it but does not enforce it. If that sheet also carries a scale caption somewhere, the app will read it, apply it, and price the sheet. On a genuinely NTS drawing, no length on it means anything.

05Line styles

A "style" is one family of identical linework — same colour, same weight, same dash pattern, same CAD layer. Every line on the sheet that matches gets grouped into one row.

Each row shows you the total footage, how many separate lines are in the family, its colour swatch, its CAD layer name where there is one, and the tick box that decides whether it counts.

ControlWhat it does
Filter line types…Narrows the list as you type — by name, by class, by colour word.
tick boxThe gate on everything. Untick a style and its footage vanishes from the takeoff, the tally, the CSV and the export.
name boxPre-filled from the legend or CAD layer. Whatever is here is the line item's identity everywhere downstream.
LF / EABill this family by footage, or by counting pieces.

Careful with "all"

all switches on rows the detector deliberately left off — hatch, texture, text strokes — and rows the trade filter is hiding. It is a blunt instrument. If you use it, go back and re-cull.

Names are remembered

Name a line type once and that name comes back on every sheet of the document, and on the same document next week. Prices and trade assignments go further: they are remembered across every document in this browser, keyed on the exact name text. See Saved work.

06Declaring your trades

If you only do wet utilities, you should not have to untick fencing on every sheet. The scope bar asks Whose scope is this? and lets you say.

Pick your trades from the chips and the app unticks the line types that clearly belong to trades you didn't pick. Every row it set aside can be put back with one click, and show everything restores the whole sheet exactly.

Verified

A trade declaration is a view, not a decision about the sheet. Leaving the page and coming back, or pressing show everything, returns every last foot. This was measured on a real sheet: 178.0k ft across 165 rows → declare two trades → 64.9k across 126 → page away and back → 64.9k / 126 → show everything → 178.0k ft / 165 rows.

Where it over-reaches

Trade matching is a plain text search inside the layer name, so a layer called AGGREGATE contains the letters GATE and can be pulled toward fencing. Scan what got set aside before you trust the reduced sheet — the bar tells you how many rows it touched.

07The itemized takeoff

The priced bill for one sheet. Every ticked line type is rolled up by name into one row, grouped under seven fixed trades, with a subtotal each and a total at the bottom.

Five columns: the name, a count, the quantity, your unit rate, and the extended amount. Only two of those are detected — the count and the quantity. The name, the trade, the price and any quantity you retype are yours.

Overriding a quantity

Click a quantity box, type your number, press Tab. The box goes bold with a coloured border, and hovering it tells you what detected figure you replaced. Clear the box to go back.

The most important caveat in this handbook

A footage override you type changes the panel, the trade subtotals, the TOTAL and the CSV — and never reaches the exported JSON or the Report PDF, which are recomputed from the raw geometry. Same for cubic-yard rows from the calculators, and for symbol counts from the Count panel.

So the report can carry a different number from the one you corrected on screen, under the same line-item name, with no warning anywhere. On any sheet where you overrode a quantity, use the CSV as your deliverable.

When a row is drawn twice

Plans sometimes draw the same run twice — two chains over identical geometry. Where that happens the row grows a note reading sum … union … drawn by more than one of these N lines, showing both figures and picking neither. use union writes the de-duplicated figure into the quantity box.

Treat "no note" as "no overlap detected". The test is an exact geometry match — a duplicate drawn a fraction of a point to the side does not register.

Money on screen has no commas

The panel renders totals with no thousands separator, so eighty-four thousand three hundred and twenty dollars appears as $84320. Commas do appear in the report PDF. Read carefully at a glance.

The summary page is not a project total

The report's SUM OF APPROVED SHEETS adds each approved sheet together with no de-duplication. A run drawn on four sheets is in that number four times. The PDF prints this caveat itself, under the total.

Re-detecting a page does not invalidate an export you already approved. The report is built from whatever was last approved, however old. If you change a sheet materially, approve it again.

09Earthwork, Trench and Structure

These turn linework into cubic yards. They are the most useful tools in the app and the easiest to get a badly wrong number out of, because a volume has no obvious sanity check by eye.

Earthwork

Reads the sheet's labelled contours, sorts them into an existing surface and a proposed surface, builds a model of each, and reports cut, fill and net.

Earthwork has a consent step: nothing reaches your takeoff until you press Add to takeoff. Press it again to take the rows back out.

Check every contour row

The EXIST / PROP choice on each contour is a guess made from line weight — thinner than average defaults to existing, heavier to proposed. On any sheet that doesn't follow that convention, the guess is a coin toss, and swapping two surfaces inverts cut and fill completely.

A row whose note says its label was 999pt away did not have its elevation read from a label at all — that number was inherited from another line. Check those against the drawing individually.

Trench

Excavation and bedding volumes for a pipe run.

No consent step

Unlike Earthwork and Structure, pressing Calculate trench writes four priced rows straight into your takeoff — there is no "add" button to press and no obvious way to take them back out. Calculating a second time for a different pipe replaces the first run's rows rather than adding to them.

Structure

Building footprint: mass excavation, perimeter layback dig and backfill, slab prep. Has a consent step.

Two things it does not do

Backfill is set exactly equal to excavation — the volume the foundation wall and footing occupy is not deducted. And running Structure on a sheet where you also ran Earthwork double-counts the building excavation, because the contour surfaces already cover that footprint.

Disclosure reaches the report unevenly

Earthwork's caveat is printed on the Report PDF. Trench and Structure cubic yards print with no disclosure at all. If those rows are in a bid, say so yourself.

10Count, Callouts and Whole job

Count finds repeated symbols — window marks, fixtures, grid bubbles — and counts them. It reports the largest groups on the sheet, needs a symbol to appear at least three times before it will report it at all, and counts every printed tag, not only the ones in your trade. On the set it was measured against, a little over half of what it counted was in scope.

Callouts pairs each leader line with its text and the linework it points at — useful for checking that a labelled run is the run you think it is.

Whole job rolls every mark code up across the whole document, per sheet and in total. Across sheets shows labels appearing on more than one approved sheet with the footage each contributes — this is how you catch a run you have counted twice. It reads the approved snapshot, not the sheet as it is now.

Stale panels

Switching to a page that has not been detected leaves these panels open showing the previous sheet's rows. Detect the page before reading them.

11Analyze

Sends a picture of the sheet and some of its text to an AI model and asks what the sheet is for and which line types an estimator would actually measure. It comes back as a list of proposals.

Nothing it suggests touches your takeoff until you press Accept on that individual row, and every accepted row keeps its own Undo. There is deliberately no "apply all" — one click was measured to keep only about a fifth of a sheet's drawn length and discard the rest.

Hover the small measured chip on any row to see how often that kind of suggestion was right, measured over 33 pages read three times each.

The dangerous row

turn OFF removes footage from your takeoff, and about one time in six it removes real work — measured at 248 of 1,471 such suggestions dropping a wall, footing, slab, curb or pipe layer.

These rows are also the only ones that never carry a reason, because of how the model is asked for them. Silence there is an absence of evidence, not confidence. Each one shows the exact footage it would delete, in red. Read that number, look at the linework, and when in doubt leave it ticked — an over-count you can see beats an under-count you cannot.

Do not take the model's scale

The scale it reads is wrong roughly one time in eight on scanned sheets. The banner deliberately refuses to apply it and prints the engine's reading beside it for comparison. If they disagree, resolve it from the drawing, not from the model.

Likewise, pressing Re-read sheet and getting the same answer is not confirmation. On the pages with a confirmed title, three were wrong in all three runs.

12Saved work and fresh demos

Your labels, ticks, prices, trades, scales and quantity overrides are remembered in the browser and come back when you reopen a document — including after you close the tab entirely. A Saved work · N button appears in the header showing how much is being held. On a seat that has never been used, it does not appear at all.

Clicking it offers two options, and the difference matters:

ActionClearsLeaves
Reset this document This PDF's labels, ticks, scales and overrides — and the service's cached detection for it, so the next run is genuinely cold. Your shared price list and trade assignments, and every other document.
Forget everything All of the above for every document, plus the shared prices and trades. Cached detections.
For a client demo, use "Forget everything"

Reset this document deliberately keeps your price list. Retype a name that matches a previously priced one and the old rate reappears and extends into the total — which in front of a client is exactly the pre-baked number you were trying to avoid.

Reset tells you what it will destroy

Before deleting, the dialog lists real counts — cached detections, page renders, and, in capitals, any approved sheet exports and report PDF. Those two are your work, not a cache. Download the report first if you still need it.

13Before you bid

Everything here produces a number that looks completely normal on screen. These are the checks that catch them. Ordered by what it costs to miss.

Wrong numberA typed footage override never reaches the report

You correct a quantity, the panel and the CSV show your figure, and the exported JSON and Report PDF show the uncorrected detected one — same line item, different number, no warning.

CheckOn any sheet where you overrode a quantity, deliver the CSV. If the report must be the deliverable, fix the quantity by unticking or erasing linework instead of typing over it.
Wrong numberAn override applies to every sheet, not just this one

Overrides are stored per document, not per page. Override a name to 24 on sheet 3 and every row with that name on sheet 5 also reads 24, whatever is actually drawn there.

CheckAfter paging to a new sheet, look for any bold, coloured-border quantity box you did not type on this sheet. Hover it — the tooltip names the detected figure it is hiding.
Wrong numberPrices carry across every job in the browser

Rates are keyed on the exact line-item name with no document scope. A rate entered on last month's job reappears on today's the moment a row carries the same name, already extended into the total.

CheckOn a new job, scan the price column before trusting the total. Anything already filled in was not entered on this job. Only Forget everything clears them.
Wrong numberA plan-and-profile sheet bills the graph as construction

A profile is a graph — stationing across, elevation up, usually at an exaggerated vertical scale. The app treats the whole sheet as one drawing, so the graph's grid lines and its plotted surfaces become billable linework at the plan's scale.

CheckOn any sheet with a profile box, untick everything inside it, or take the plan off separately from the profile.
Wrong numberEarthwork and Structure on the same sheet double-count

The contour surfaces already cover the building footprint, so adding Structure's mass excavation on top counts the same dirt twice. Structure's backfill also omits the volume the foundation itself occupies.

CheckUse one or the other on a given footprint, not both.
Wrong numberA plausible scale typo is not caught

The sanity band only refuses the absurd. Typing 200 where you meant 20 is accepted and makes every length on the sheet ten times too big.

CheckAfter typing a scale, look at one known dimension on the drawing — a road width, a building face — and confirm the app agrees with the printed dimension string.
MisleadingA green confidence chip certifies the scale, nothing else

Green says a scale number was read and corroborated. It says nothing about whether the linework is right, whether the names are right, whether the quantities are right, or whether the earthwork volumes are right.

CheckRead green as "the ruler is probably right", never as "the sheet is right".
MisleadingThe trade heading adds cubic yards to linear feet and calls it feet

On any trade containing a calculator row, the footage figure in the group heading is a meaningless mixture. The individual rows and all the money are fine.

CheckIgnore the heading's footage whenever that trade contains a CY or SF row. Read the rows, or the CSV.
MisleadingThe running tally can describe a sheet you are no longer on

Move to a page that has not been detected and the itemized table empties while the tally bar keeps displaying the previous sheet's footage and dollar total.

CheckA blank table with a populated tally means detect the page first.
MisleadingOn a sheet with no scale, "feet" are really points

The column heading changes to pt and prices stop extending on measured rows, but the numbers still look like quantities.

CheckIf the column reads PT rather than FEET, stop and fix the scale before entering anything.
MisleadingTyping 0 does not zero a row

Zero and negative overrides are ignored, so the detected figure quietly comes back and the row looks deliberately set.

CheckTo remove a row, untick its line type. Do not try to zero the quantity.
MisleadingHand edits to linework do not survive a page change

Labels, ticks and prices survive. Geometry edits — erasing a line, splitting one out — are thrown away when you change pages or re-detect, without warning.

CheckFinish and approve a sheet before leaving it.

What none of this covers

These are measured against CAD layer names and hand-confirmed counts on drawings we have. They are not measured against a real estimator's finished takeoff on a job we had never seen, because we do not yet have enough matched pairs of drawings-plus-estimate to say that honestly. Until we do, treat these numbers as "the software finds what is drawn", not "the software agrees with your estimator".
