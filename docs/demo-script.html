CORE Demo Script

A twelve-minute run for a client call: which drawings to open, what to say at each beat, and which cautions to raise before they find them.

01The one idea you are selling

Not "it measures drawings." Every competitor says that. What you have that they don't is that it tells you when it doesn't know.

An estimator's real fear is not slow software. It's a number that looks right and isn't — because that one goes into a bid and nobody finds it until the job is losing money. Your whole demo should be built around the moment the app refuses to give an answer.

"Most takeoff tools will happily give you a number for anything you point them at. The problem is you can't tell which numbers are solid and which are guesses. So we built ours to show its work — and to stop and say 'I don't know' rather than hand you something that looks confident and is wrong."

Everything below is in service of that sentence. If you only get five minutes, do Beat 3 and Beat 4 and nothing else.

02Before the call — five minutes

  1. Start the service: bash detection-engine/run-service.sh
  2. Open http://127.0.0.1:8100 — the numbers, not localhost, which is seconds slower on every click.
  3. Clear the seat. If Saved work · N is showing in the header, click it and choose Forget everything. Otherwise your prices and labels from a previous session appear on their drawing and the demo looks staged.
  4. Pre-detect the two sheets in Beats 3 and 4 once, so the demo isn't ninety seconds of waiting. Then, if you want to show a cold run, use Reset this document live — it clears the cache in front of them and proves nothing was pre-baked.
  5. Have the client's own drawing open in another tab if they sent one. Ending on their sheet is worth more than everything before it.
Two things that will bite you

Prices are shared across every job in the browser. Reset this document deliberately keeps them. For a clean demo use Forget everything.

Calculator rows don't survive a page change. If you run Earthwork and then flick to another sheet, the cubic yards are gone when you come back. Finish a sheet before leaving it.

04The run, beat by beat

  1. Open a drawing

    Barn Civil · 30 seconds

    Paste the path, press Open, pick sheet 3, press Detect lines.

    "This is a real civil sheet from a real job — nothing prepared. One button. It's reading every line on the page, working out which ones are the same kind of line, and finding the scale."

  2. The result lands

    15 seconds · let them look

    Status line reads 1840 lines in 280 styles · scale 1 inch = 30 ft. (text, matches the drawn bar).

    "Eighteen hundred lines, sorted into two hundred and eighty types. That's the part that takes an estimator an afternoon with a mouse."

    Don't rush past this. Let the number sit for a beat.

  3. The confidence banner — show your work

    90 seconds · the first of the two key moments

    Point at the green banner. It reads, in full:

    On screen

    "Scale confirmed two ways: 1 inch = 30 ft. The sheet prints it in words (1 inch = 30 ft.) and the ruler drawn on the sheet measures the same thing. Two independent readings agree."

    "Checked against the sheet's own figures. 1 area printed on this page agrees with what we measured at this scale, within 2%."

    "Here's what I mean by showing its work. It didn't just read the scale off the title block — it also measured the ruler drawn on the sheet, and checked both against an area the drafter had already labelled. Three sources, and they agree."

    "Scale is the one number that, if it's wrong, makes everything on the sheet wrong by the same factor. So it's the one we refuse to be casual about."

  4. The refusal — switch to the Town Pump sheet

    60 seconds · the moment that wins the room

    Open the Town Pump site map, detect it. It finds 1,607 lines and goes red.

    On screen

    "No scale could be read off this sheet. Until one is set, nothing here can be quoted in feet, square feet or cubic yards — the lengths below are paper measurements of the drawing, not real-world distances."

    Then point at the itemized table: the column heading has changed from feet to pt, and the price boxes do nothing.

    "Same software, different drawing. It found sixteen hundred lines here perfectly well — but this sheet doesn't say what it was drawn to, so it will not give me a footage. Look: the column doesn't even say 'feet' any more, and it won't let me price it."

    "That's deliberate. A tool that guessed here would hand me a bid-ready number that's wrong by whatever factor it guessed, and I'd never know. I'd rather it stop and make me do ten seconds of work."

    Now type the scale in and watch it come alive.

    "I read the scale off the drawing, type it once — and now every one of those sixteen hundred lines is in feet, and it'll price. Once it knows the scale it's fully automatic. It just won't pretend to know."

  5. Cull to your scope

    Back on Barn Civil · 90 seconds

    Two things to show. First, the sheet arrives with most line types already switched on and already named from the drawing's own legend and CAD layers.

    "It doesn't start blank — it starts with its best guess already made, using the sheet's own legend. Your job is to take things out, not put them in. That's a much faster job."

    Click a line type in the list. The row is marked ISOLATED and the canvas shows only that one.

    "Click any line type and it isolates on the drawing, so you can see exactly what you're about to bill for before you bill for it. Escape puts everything back."

    Then the scope bar: declare a couple of trades and watch the sheet narrow.

    "If you only do wet and dry utilities, tell it once and it sets aside everything that clearly isn't yours. And show everything brings back every last foot — it's a way of looking at the sheet, not a decision about it."

  6. Correct a number and price it

    90 seconds · the second key moment

    Click into a quantity box. Show that the detected figure is in the box at full precision, and the arrows nudge it.

    "The measured number is right here, and if it's slightly off you can just nudge it — the arrows step by a sensible amount for the size of the run."

    Type a different figure. The row grows a sentence: overridden — this sheet uses 18,000.0 ft; the engine detected 25,027.8 ft.

    "And when you overrule it, it says so, in words, right there — and keeps its own number next to yours so anyone reviewing the sheet later can see exactly what was measured and what was judgement."

    Type a unit rate and let the extended total fill in.

  7. Export — and prove the number travels

    60 seconds · the closer

    Press Approve & export. Read the status line aloud — it reports how many typed quantities went into the export. Then open the Report PDF.

    "Here's the part I'd want to see if I were you. The number I corrected is in the report — starred, with the measured figure printed underneath it. So the document that goes to your client can't quietly disagree with the screen you approved."

    Why this beat matters

    Until this week the report carried the detector's number instead of the corrected one. On one row of one sheet that was a $70,278 difference. Fixed and verified — but it's the reason this beat is in the script: it's the failure mode estimators are actually afraid of, and you can show it working.

  8. Finish on their drawing

    However long it takes

    Open something they sent. Do not pre-run it.

    "Let's do one of yours, cold, so you can see what it does with a sheet nobody's tuned it for."

    If it does something wrong, say so out loud before they do. See the next section.

05Cautions to raise yourself

Volunteering a limitation buys more trust than hiding it costs. An estimator who catches you overselling stops believing the good parts too.

"I'll tell you where it's weak, because you'd find it in a week anyway and I'd rather you hear it from me."

Say these, in roughly this order

  • Scale is the thing to check first, every time. "It's the one number that makes everything else wrong if it's off. Green means two sources agreed. Anything else, check the sheet yourself before you price."
  • Earthwork volumes are the least trustworthy thing in the app. "Cut and fill is built from the contours it can read, and it defaults each contour to existing or proposed by line weight — which is a guess. Check that assignment before you believe the cubic yards. On the one job where we had the client's own estimate to compare, it ran high."
  • Counting sees everything, not just your trade. "It'll find every repeated symbol on the sheet, which means it over-counts if the sheet has other trades' tags on it. It's a starting point you edit down, not a final number."
  • Scans are harder than CAD. "A native PDF still carries the drafter's own layer names — that's the strongest signal we get. A scan is just pixels. It still works, it's just less certain, and it'll tell you so."
  • A sheet that mixes a plan with details has more than one scale. "No single number is right for the whole page. Take the plan off at its scale and the details separately."
If they ask about accuracy — the honest framing

Do not quote a single percentage. Say what was measured against what:

"On the sheets we have CAD layer names for, it traces about 99% of the ink and files about 92% of it under the right name. On a scanned set where we hand-counted 285 openings it found 99.3% of them. What we can't yet tell you is how closely it matches a finished estimate on a job we've never seen — we don't have enough paired drawings-and-estimates to say that honestly. Which is exactly why we're asking for them."

06Questions you will get

"Does it replace my estimator?"

"No — it replaces the two hours he spends tracing lines with a mouse. He still decides what's in scope, what it's called, and what it costs. Those are the parts that need him."

"What if it gets a number wrong?"

"You change it, and the change follows through to the export and the printed report, marked as yours with the measured figure beside it. Nothing is locked, and nothing changes silently."

"Will it work on our drawings?"

"Send me a set and I'll run it in front of you. That's a better answer than anything I can promise." Then actually do it — cold, on the call if you can.

"What about our old scanned plans?"

"They work — that's Bill's set, all photocopies. It reads them out of the image instead of the CAD data. Less certain, and it says when it's less certain."

They open a plan-and-profile sheet and the numbers look mad.

"Good catch, and that's a known one. That box at the bottom is a graph, not a plan — different vertical scale — and right now it measures the graph's grid as if it were pipe. Until we fix it, take the plan off separately and leave the profile out. I'd rather tell you that than have you find it in a bid."

"How is this different from what we already have?"

"Most of it isn't, honestly — everyone can draw a line and total it. The difference is what happens when the drawing is ambiguous. Ours stops. I'll show you." Go to Beat 4.
