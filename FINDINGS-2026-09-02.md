# 2026-09-02 — corrections to claims made earlier in this session

## "every volume identical" is true of the GATE, not of the product

Commit c6c1099 (`gate: re-accept earthwork_sig ... every volume identical`)
and the merge commit that followed it are correctly scoped in their bodies
and OVERCLAIM in their subject lines. What was proven is that all 29 pinned
gate cases returned float-identical cut/fill/net across the re-accept. What
was NOT proven, and is false, is that no volume the product computes moved:
the same session's gap-OCR change took the DEM outline sheet from 0 rows and
an HTTP 400 to 21 rows and a volume 44% low on cut and 49% high on fill.

The gate pins two sheets and the offsets {0, 0.5, 0.75}. An adversary
demonstrated the general shape on 2026-09-02: a change that only fires on an
input outside those cases (a 4-inch topsoil strip, `existing_offset_ft`
0.3333) moved a real bid — fill 8550.5 -> 7267.9 through the product route —
while the gate printed "25 cases passed, 0 failed" and the only failure lines
were the mandatory `earthwork_sig moved`. The honest re-accept commit that
follows such a change would say "every volume identical" and be right about
all 87 gate fields and wrong about the bid.

So: a re-accept commit may say "every pinned case is identical". It may not
say "no volume moved" unless the change is one the pinned cases actually
exercise. When re-accepting, name the input shapes the 29 cases cover and
say which shapes the change touches that they do not.

## What the gate does and does not cover, as of this commit

Covered: any .py under `takeoff_engine/`, plus `server.py` and
`provision_demo.py` (earthwork_sig, 26 files); stale bytecode; the product
route's defaults AND typed `cell_ft` / `surface_model`; layer offsets
{0, 0.5, 0.75} ft; page scales {20, 30, 40} ft/in; spot counts {0, 10, 46};
two sheets (Oncor C-04, Stice p0); the fixture files by sha256; the chain
fingerprint `engine_sig`; and, under `--require`, parked entries, skipped
truth cases and out-of-tree import hooks.

NOT covered, and known: the HTTP layer above the route function; what
`webui/app.js` puts in the body (which rows and spots, how the boundary is
drawn); `/earthwork/map` and `/earthwork/zone`; the elevation readers behind
`/earthwork/suggest` (so a change to WHICH rows reach the volume is invisible
here — that is exactly the gap the gap-OCR change fell through); scale
DETECTION (the fixtures carry a scale, they do not read one); the ledger's
contents; the approve/export path; and any input shape outside the list
above.

## Contour coverage and the contour review surface (2026-09-02, later)

WHAT CHANGED. `/earthwork/suggest` now returns `contour_coverage` -
takeoff_engine.earthwork.contour_coverage, a pure function: how many contour
lines the sheet draws, how many carry an elevation, by which mechanism, how
much footage each is, the sheet's own contour interval, and the chain id of
every contour line with no elevation at all. The earthwork panel renders the
statement above the rows, beside the calculated number, and on the printed
report - one wording (`ewCoverageText`), three surfaces, the arrangement
`ewBasisNote` / `ewCautionText` already use. The rows themselves gained a
filter bar with one chip per class of evidence, a "most doubtful first"
order, `line N` as the row's name, and a `revealChain()` click that goes list
-> sheet.

WHAT IT FIXED. Nothing in the app stated a contour DENOMINATOR. Oncor C-04
draws 368 contour lines; 150 carry an elevation and the panel showed those
150 in elevation order with no way to know about the other 218. The rows a
volume is actually wrong about were unfindable: 551.2 on line 168 is a
`BW` wall spot 2.7 pt away and the sheet prints 550 on that contour
(out/verify/2026-09-02/ew-contour-review/11-src-line168-551p2-BW.png, source
PDF at 600 dpi). 23 of C-04's 150 rows carry a number that is not a step on
the sheet's own 1 ft interval; the sweep is 1 chip click + 23 row clicks,
counted by driving the browser.

WHAT IS STILL OPEN.
 * The denominator is the families the legend named contours OR that produced
   a row. Everything else is reported as `unclassed` and is in neither count
   - on Barn civil p2 that is 1,000 chains against 351. Naming those families
   is a separate job.
 * Coverage across the corpus is far worse than C-04: Barn civil p3 178
   contour lines and ZERO elevations, Plans2 C4 / Ernies C4 no identifiable
   contour family at all, BelleFarms ROAD GRADING 40 of 334. The statement
   now says so; the reader has not improved.
 * The off-interval rank is a QUESTION, never a correction - nothing is
   auto-fixed and no volume moved. A row that is off the step and right is
   still shown as off the step.
 * `APPROVE_NOTES_MAX` moved 6 -> 7 (server.py `body.notes[:7]`) so the
   coverage note could not push the last caveat off the report.
