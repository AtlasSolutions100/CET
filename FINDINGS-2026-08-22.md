## F73 — The architect already PRINTS the counts; Bill's job is verification [PRODUCT-DEFINING]

Found while checking F72's remark that A830's QUANTITY column has six rows.

The Aqua Vista WINDOW SCHEDULE (page index 0, rect 370,120-665,870 pt) has a
**printed QUANTITY column**: W1L 148, W1R 15, W2L 12, W3L 2, W4 8, W5 3. Same clean
type as the rest of the table - the architect put those numbers there, not Bill.

Bill then counted every floor by hand anyway, and drew a **tick beside five of the six
figures**. He did not tick W5. His written total is **185**; the schedule totals
**188**. Capture: `ANNOTATED_schedule_already_counts.png`.

**This reframes the feature.** The count is not the deliverable - it is already on the
sheet. What Bill is paid for, and what carries his risk, is **reconciling the printed
schedule against the drawings**: if the schedule is wrong and he orders from it, he
eats the cost. So the product feature is:

> read the schedule's printed quantity, independently count the tags on the plans,
> and report agreement or disagreement per type.

Two consequences that matter:

1. **It lowers the accuracy bar in a useful way.** A detector does not have to be
   perfect to be valuable - it has to be good enough to flag a disagreement. When the
   independent count agrees with the printed schedule, that agreement is itself
   evidence both are right. F72's 129/185 is not "71% of a deliverable"; it is a
   detector that currently cannot be trusted to contradict the schedule, which is a
   different and clearer bar.
2. **It makes the document layer mandatory, not optional.** Reconciliation needs the
   schedule table on page 0 AND the tags on pages 1-5 in one operation. Today every
   page is an island (16 of 18 job endpoints are page-scoped).

Also note the counts now have **three independent sources** that agree: Bill's
per-sheet tallies, his master table on the schedule sheet, and the architect's printed
QUANTITY column. Recorded in `ground-truth/aqua-vista-marked_p*.json`.

**Question for Bill (do not guess - CLAUDE.md §4):** why no tick on W5? Either he
could not find those 3 openings on the plans - a real discrepancy worth flagging - or
W5 is out of his scope. The W5 row carries "Ruskin" in its MODEL column where the
other rows read "VPI / ENDURANCE / IGU", and the W5 NOTES cell reads "N/A" where the
others carry the IBC 1015.8(2) fall-protection note. **Inference, not established:**
that pattern reads like W5 is a louver rather than a glazed window, which would put it
outside a glazing estimator's scope. Ask; do not assume.

## F74 — Scale reader returned "SCALE 2" on Guide civil: a 25x volume error [FIXED, 67cff1c]

`_PAT_KEYWORD` had every group but `<feet>` optional, so the bare word SCALE followed
by any integer matched. On **Guide civil engine page index 2**, `_row_text()` joins the
page to `... GRAPHIC SCALE 2% 2% W CONSTRUCTION ...` → read as 1" = 2 ft, and because
keyword hits sort first in `_choose` it beat the sheet's own "1 inch = 10 ft.".

Live app, identical 46 contour rows and identical default surfaces, only the scale
differing (`CR12_scale_cost_earthwork.png`):

| | cut | fill | net | area |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 224 CY | 29.3 CY | -194.7 CY | 2,212 SF |
| after | **5,697.2 CY** | 741.1 CY | -4,956.1 CY | **55,812 SF** |

**25.4x on cut, 25.2x on area** — elevations are already in feet, so a 5x linear scale
error squares into volume. It reached earthwork **silently**; nothing in the UI would
have shown an estimator the scale was wrong. Page index 3 escaped only by luck: there
the same pattern matched `SCALE 2026`, the sheet date, which the plausibility guard
already rejected.

Verified with `detect_scale` across **323 corpus pages** — exactly one line changed.
`regress` does not exercise scale at all, so that sweep, not the gate, is the safety
argument. **Use the same method for any future scale change.**
