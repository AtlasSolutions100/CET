## 3. The cut/fill build plan

### What was decided, and on what evidence

**Christian's contour-band method is not being adopted.** Measured against the
Stice sheet's own printed EARTHWORK QUANTITIES table (RAW row: cut 2,236,137 /
fill 1,631,080 CY) it runs −25.99% / −30.36%, where the shipped model runs
−9.86% / −9.01%. On the 20 scoreable printed spot elevations on Oncor C-04 it
is the least accurate of three models (0.369 ft vs 0.266 ft mean absolute
error). On a closed-form synthetic dome it slopes downhill at the peak. Its
one favourable row was traced to a defect, not to the method working.

**But Christian is right that something is wrong** — the shipped model is
9–12% low against every ground truth we have, consistently, never high.

**Per-pixel resolution is not the problem.** Refining the grid from 4.00 ft to
0.25 ft cells (5,248 → 1,340,128 cells) moves the answer **under 1%**. This
answers his one-pixel-cylinder argument directly and is worth telling him: the
error is not in the sampling, it is in what the model does *between* contours.

**The actual cause is area never integrated over.** You can only subtract two
surfaces where you have both. On Oncor, inline labels seeded elevations on
**3 of ~34** proposed contours, so most of the site has no proposed grade and
contributes exactly zero. Consistently low, never high, is the signature of
omission rather than noise. Christian named this without knowing it:
*"everywhere where there's a proposed grade and not anywhere else."*

### The design (Koby's, worked out 2026-08-31)

The number to trust is not the volume. It is **how much of the site the volume
speaks for.**

1. **Coverage is the headline.** The fraction of *the boundary the operator
   drew* where both surfaces are defined. Not "of the site" — the app cannot
   know where the site ends. Of the polygon, which is exact.
2. **Shade the computed region on the sheet.** This is the strongest part of
   the design. A sentence saying "some of this may be missing" gets scrolled
   past; a shaded shape with a bite out of it is a finding, not a caveat.
3. **Yellow until confirmed.** Below full coverage the volume renders
   unconfirmed. It is visible and workable — refusing to show it would block
   real work on sheets that are only ever 70% readable — but it **cannot enter
   the takeoff or the report** until a person accepts it.
4. **Confirmation is a decision, not a dismissal.** "Accepted at 62% coverage"
   is persisted, ledgered and **printed on the report** beside the volume, in
   exactly the sense this app already separates decisions from edits. When the
   number is questioned three weeks later the answer is on the paper.
5. **The boundary is also the input.** Draw it, then set existing / proposed /
   both for that region. This is how a flat pad that exists only as a spot
   grade and a note gets into the calculation at all. Typed values stay marked
   as typed everywhere they propagate.
6. **The existing disclaimers stay underneath.** Especially the datum
   conflict: coverage can read 100% and every cubic yard still be wrong if the
   sheet mixes assumed datum and NAVD88 between layers.

Why this answers the original question — "how would someone with no
experience know it's correct?" It doesn't claim correctness. It shows what it
measured, shows what it couldn't, and makes you sign for the difference. That
is checkable without being an estimator. "Trust the algorithm" never is.

### Build order

**Phase 0 — ground truth. Blocks honest measurement of everything after it.**
The project contains exactly **one** printed volume table. `ground-truth/`'s
33 files are door arcs, window tags and shear walls — no grading sheet.
Harvest printed EARTHWORK QUANTITIES tables from sheets already in the corpus
into `ground-truth/`. Target five or more. You cannot tune what you cannot
measure against, and every accuracy claim after this is unfalsifiable without
it.

**Phase 1 — coverage.** Compute it against the drawn boundary; shade the
covered region; put the fraction at the top of the earthwork panel. Starts
after the repair lands, because it touches the same panel.

**Phase 2 — the confirmation gate.** Yellow state, takeoff/report blocked
until accepted, acceptance recorded as a decision and printed.

**Phase 3 — the boundary as an input surface.** Set existing / proposed /
both over a drawn region; provenance travels with the value.

**Phase 4 — validate on the client file.** Run the whole thing on the sheet
where we hold the client's own numbers. Expectation to test, not assume:
coverage explains most of the 9–12%. Report per `CLAUDE.md` §2 — source,
expected, measured, never a bare percentage.

**Phase 5 — only then, the interpolator.** Not before. While coverage is the
bottleneck, tuning interpolation is polishing the part that isn't broken.

**Not doing:** the contour-band method; per-pixel grid refinement.

at once is a merge conflict, not throughput. Separate branches, one owner per
file, merge at declared points.

The real risk is not conflicts, which are avoidable. It is **divergent
assumptions** — two readings of what "where there's a proposed grade and not
anywhere else" means as a concrete mask, producing two plausible and different
numbers. This document exists to kill that. If you are an agent joining this
work and something here is ambiguous, ask rather than choosing.
